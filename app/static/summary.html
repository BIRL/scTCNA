Malignant vs non-malignant noise across cancers

  • The question: over all genes, are malignant cells noisier than the non-malignant cells of the same patient? Every patient gives one value per measure, and the patients are then tested against zero, so the patient is the unit of replication.
  • Raw log2(CV malignant / CV non-malignant): for each gene detected in at least 10% of cells of both groups, the log2 ratio of its CV in malignant cells to its CV in non-malignant cells (TCNA Eq. 1), then the median over genes. Above zero means malignant cells are noisier.
  • Depth-matched: all cells are downsampled to the same UMI count first, so sequencing depth cannot drive the ratio.
  • Comparison group: the comparison group is either all non-malignant cells of the same patient or one lineage (T cells, fibroblasts, ...).
  • Excess noise: how far a gene's log10 CV² in malignant cells lies above the comparison group's mean-CV² trend at the same expression level (depth-matched; median over genes), so expression level cannot drive it either.
Negative values are expected: after matching depth and expression level, malignant cells are slightly less variable than non-malignant cells in all ten cancer types; see the scTCNA manuscript.
