About scTCNA

A single-cell RNA-seq counterpart of The Cancer Noise Atlas (TCNA): the same transcriptional-noise metrics, computed on malignant and non-malignant cells of the same patients.

What scTCNA is

TCNA (Shah et al., J Mol Biol 2026) quantifies transcriptional noise in bulk RNA-seq by comparing primary tumours with solid-tissue normals across patients. scTCNA applies the same vocabulary to droplet single-cell RNA-seq: for every gene, noise is measured across the cells of one patient, separately for the Malignant compartment (cells called malignant by the atlas) and the Non-malignant compartment (all curated non-malignant lineages pooled), and the two are compared within each patient. Cancer-type values are medians across patients; every test is paired across patients.

The app has the dashboards of TCNA plus a summary view: Gene Noise Analysis (one gene across cancers, or a gene list within one cancer, with per-patient and per-cell-type detail), Top genes (ranked lists per cancer type), Tumor Heterogeneity (DEPTH and DEPTH2), a cross-cancer Heatmap and the Cross-cancer summary (is malignant-cell noise higher, patient by patient?). Every table can be downloaded as CSV and every plot as PNG (camera icon in the plot toolbar); the JSON API is listed at the bottom of this page.

Data sources

Study citations

    Primary source: 3CA, the Curated Cancer Cell Atlas (Gavish et al. 2023), which provides raw UMI counts and curated cell-type calls; malignant calls come from the atlas and are not re-derived. The TISCH2 loader (Han et al. 2023) is included in the pipeline but its entries are disabled, because TISCH2 distributes log-normalised matrices.

    Processing in brief

    • Cell cap: patient x compartment groups above 3,000 cells are randomly subsampled to 3,000 (seed 0) before QC.
    • QC: cells with ≥ 200 genes, ≥ 500 UMIs and ≤ 20% mitochondrial counts; genes detected in ≥ 10 cells; Scrublet doublets removed per sample; patient x compartment groups with fewer than 20 cells dropped.
    • Normalisation: CP10K for the gene noise metrics; log2(CP10K + 1) per cell and log2(CPM + 1) per pseudo-bulk for DEPTH / DEPTH2.
    • Groups: metrics are computed per gene within patient x compartment groups (primary unit), within patient x cell-type groups with ≥ 50 cells (lineage level), and on all cells pooled per compartment (a pseudoreplication diagnostic).
    • Expressed genes: detection rate (median over patients) ≥ 10% in both compartments; only these enter tests, top lists and the per-patient tables.

    Metric definitions

    For gene g within one group of n cells, with xig the CP10K value in cell i:

    CVCV = SD / meanTCNA's coefficient of variation (stored as a ratio; TCNA's web code reports CV x 100). Undefined (blank) when the mean is 0.
    CV²CV² = (SD / mean)²
    MADMAD = (1/n) Σi |xig - mean|Mean absolute deviation about the mean, the TCNA definition (not the median absolute deviation).
    FanoFano = variance / mean = SD² / meanscTCNA addition; Poisson sampling noise gives Fano = 1.

    Statistics

    • Per gene and cancer type, the per-patient Dgp (at least 5 patients with both compartments) are tested with a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test (zero differences discarded).
    • Effect sizes: the median per-patient log2 ratio and the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation r = (R+ - R-) / (R+ + R-) (+1 when every patient is noisier in malignant cells).
    • Benjamini-Hochberg q-values per cancer type over the expressed genes that were tested; q < 0.05 is highlighted in the plots.
    • The same paired test (at least 3 patients) compares the per-patient DEPTH / DEPTH2 medians and pseudo-bulk scores between compartments.

    Malignant versus non-malignant noise, patient by patient

    The per-gene comparison above asks which genes differ. The Cross-cancer summary asks whether malignant cells are noisier overall. For every patient and every reference group (all non-malignant cells, or one non-malignant lineage with at least 50 cells), genes detected in at least 10% of cells of both groups are compared and the median over genes is kept, giving one number per patient:

    • log2 CV ratio (raw): median over genes of log2(CVmal/CVref), TCNA Eq. 1.
    • log2 CV ratio (depth-matched): the same after downsampling every cell to the common UMI depth, so that deeper-sequenced cells do not look less noisy.
    • Excess noise: median over genes of log10(CV²mal) minus the reference group's LOWESS mean-CV² trend evaluated at the malignant gene's mean (depth-matched; genes outside the reference's expression range are skipped). It removes both the mean dependence of CV and the depth difference.

    Each measure is tested against zero across patients with a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test (at least 5 patients). These contrasts are an scTCNA addition; the raw ratio against the pooled non-malignant compartment is also affected by its mixture of lineages, which is why single lineages are shown as well.

    DEPTH and DEPTH2

    Both tumour-heterogeneity scores follow the R packages used by TCNA and are applied to log2-transformed expression. A "sample" is either a single cell or a patient x compartment pseudo-bulk profile (summed counts, log2(CPM + 1)). Genes enter the scores when detected in ≥ 1% of the dataset's cells.

    ScoreDefinitionReference
    DEPTHDEPTHs = SDg[ (xgs - μgref)² ]
    SD across genes of the squared deviation from the reference mean; the reference is the mean over non-malignant cells (per cell) or non-malignant pseudo-bulks (per sample), which is why non-malignant pseudo-bulks have no DEPTH value.    		DEPTH algorithm, WangX-Lab/DEPTH (Li M et al., Commun Biol 2020)
    DEPTH2DEPTH2s = SDg[ |zgs| ],  zgs = (xgs - μg) / σg
    Genes z-scored across samples (sample SD; a constant gene gets z = 0 and still counts); the score is the SD across genes of |z|. No normal reference needed.    		Song D, Wang X, J Transl Med 2022 (WangX-Lab/DEPTH2)

    Assumptions and limitations

    • Tumour vs normal is replaced by malignant vs non-malignant cells of the same patient; the non-malignant compartment is a mixture of lineages (immune, stromal, normal epithelium), so composition differences between patients contribute to its noise. The per-cell-type view lets malignant cells be compared with one matched lineage.
    • One study per cancer type: cancer-type and study effects cannot be separated, and cross-cancer comparisons (heatmap) are confounded by study design, tissue handling and sequencing depth. All tests are within study and within patient.
    • Malignant calls come from the atlas (inferred copy-number aberrations) and are not re-derived; unassigned cells are excluded.
    • The melanoma study consists of metastases only; the per-patient cell cap and uniform QC mean cell numbers differ from the original publications.
    • Dropout and zero inflation inflate CV and MAD for lowly expressed genes; read the mean-corrected and depth-downsampled values alongside the raw metrics.
    • Statistical power is limited by the number of patients with both compartments (roughly 8-27 per cancer type).
    • TCNA's pathway (STRING enrichment) dashboard is not replicated and there is no custom data upload. Only 10x UMI data are used.

    JSON API

    All endpoints are GET and return JSON (NaN is serialised as null); errors carry {"detail": "..."}.

    • /api/health, /api/cancer-types, /api/datasets
    • /api/genes/search?q=&limit=, /api/genes/{gene}, /api/genes/{gene}/patients?cancer_type=, /api/genes/{gene}/celltypes?cancer_type=
    • /api/cancer-types/{ct}/top-genes?metric=&direction=&n=&expressed_only=, /api/cancer-types/{ct}/comparison?metric=
    • /api/cancer-types/{ct}/tumor-heterogeneity?score=&level=, /api/tumor-heterogeneity/summary
    • /api/noise-contrasts?cancer_type=, /api/cancer-types/{ct}/noise-contrasts
    • /api/heatmap?genes=&metric=&expressed_only=
